It preserves the reasoning, commitments and dependencies behind project work and helps teams understand downstream impact when decisions change.1. Problem and MotivationImportant project information is often created in meetings, chats and informal discussions. At the moment of discussion, a team may clearly understand what was decided, why it was decided, who accepted the follow-up work, and which tasks depend on it. As the project progresses, however, this context becomes fragmented across meeting notes, task tools and documentation systems, while the relationships among those records are often weak or completely manual.For example, a team says: “Use PostgreSQL because payment data requires transaction support. Carol will finish the schema by Friday. Bob’s API work depends on it.” Two weeks later Carol is delayed and Bob is blocked. A month later someone proposes MongoDB. The team must reconstruct why PostgreSQL was selected, whether Carol’s commitment was confirmed, which work depends on the schema, and whether the new proposal conflicts with the earlier decision.The problem is therefore not a lack of discussion or task lists. The missing layer is durable traceability among decision rationale, responsibility, dependency and later change.Project scope: DecisionTrace focuses on improving decision traceability within project teams. It does not aim to replace existing task management, communication, or documentation platforms, but instead provides a missing relationship layer between them.2. Proposed Solution and Target WorkflowDecisionTrace is an AI-assisted, human-confirmed collaboration system that converts important project discussions into structured and traceable records. AI reduces the manual effort of identifying candidate decisions, rationale, commitments and dependencies, while users remain responsible for confirming, editing or rejecting them before they become authoritative project facts.The target workflow is: (1) capture a meeting transcript, chat excerpt or manual discussion; (2) extract candidate decisions, rationale, commitments, deadlines and dependencies; (3) confirm, edit or reject the candidates; (4) link confirmed decisions, commitments and dependencies to their source; (5) search history, update status and inspect blockers; and (6) compare later proposals with confirmed history to suggest conflicts and affected work for human review.The key principle is that AI output remains a candidate until a human confirms it. AI improves the efficiency of structuring and discovery; people retain authority over formal decisions and responsibility assignments.3. Core Domain ModelThe system is centered on four core concepts. The project is not defined by the AI feature itself; its main value comes from how structured records are related and maintained over time.Decision answers “What did the team decide, and why?” and stores rationale, alternatives, owner, status and source. Commitment answers “Who explicitly agreed to do what, and by when?” and stores owner, due date, status and confirmation. Dependency answers “What work relies on this?” and stores blocked-by, blocks and related artifacts. Change answers “What changed after the original decision?” and stores superseding relationships, conflicts, impact and revisions.A Decision can create multiple Commitments. A Commitment may depend on another commitment or artifact. A later Change may supersede a Decision and trigger review of linked Dependencies. Every confirmed record keeps its source, confirmer and revision history.4. Intended Users and Major Use CasesTeam members review AI-extracted candidates, confirm personal commitments, update status and search decision context. Team leads or project managers create or revise decisions, inspect dependencies and blockers, review possible conflicts and trace change impact. New team members browse key historical decisions, understand rationale, see current owners and follow how decisions evolved.Major use cases are: UC01 import or record discussion; UC02 extract candidate decisions and commitments; UC03 review and confirm AI suggestions; UC04 create or revise a decision record; UC05 assign and track a commitment; UC06 create and inspect dependencies; UC07 detect possible conflict or change impact; and UC08 search decision history and source context.The system’s depth does not come from having many user roles. It comes from relationships, state changes, confirmation rules, version history and change handling.5. Market Context and DifferentiationAdjacent product categories already solve important parts of collaboration. Task-management tools are strong at tasks, owners and deadlines; meeting assistants are strong at transcription, summaries and action items; wikis and document tools support long-term knowledge storage; ADR-style tools preserve technical decisions and rationale.DecisionTrace’s differentiation hypothesis is not one isolated feature. It is the continuously maintained trace: Discussion → Decision → Rationale → Commitment → Dependency → Execution → Change. The system does not need to replace existing task or documentation tools; instead, it adds the relationship layer that is often missing between them.Examples of related products include Jira, Notion, Slack/Microsoft Teams, and meeting AI assistants. Jira is effective for task tracking and project execution, but usually does not preserve the reasoning behind decisions. Notion supports documentation and knowledge management, but relationships between decisions and project outcomes require manual maintenance. Slack and Microsoft Teams preserve communication history, but important decisions can become difficult to retrieve. Meeting AI assistants provide transcription and summaries, but generally do not maintain long-term dependency and responsibility traces.This is a hypothesis to validate rather than a claim that no similar capability exists. Before finalizing the SRS, the team should conduct competitor research and short user interviews to verify the gap and refine the project scope.6. Technology and AI Authority BoundaryA preliminary technology stack could use React/Next.js for the frontend, FastAPI or Node.js for the backend, PostgreSQL for core records, an LLM service for structured extraction, and embeddings/vector search for semantic linking if needed.AI may extract candidate decisions and commitments, suggest related historical records, flag possible semantic conflicts and draft rationale summaries. AI may not autonomously confirm a formal decision, assign responsibility without user confirmation, silently rewrite project history, or treat uncertain inference as fact.The design principle is “AI suggests; humans authorize.” Critical project facts must be confirmable, reversible and auditable.7. MVP Scope and Non-Functional PrioritiesThe MVP must include discussion input/import, AI extraction of candidate structured records, a human confirm/edit/reject workflow, decision–commitment–dependency relationships, searchable confirmed history, and at least one demonstrable change or conflict detection scenario.The MVP will not attempt to replace Jira/Trello, provide full chat or video conferencing, manage calendars, autonomously assign tasks, or integrate with many external platforms. This keeps the project focused on the traceability model and core business processes.Non-functional priorities include accuracy and reliability, privacy and role-based access, auditability, AI latency and cost, and maintainability. In particular, uncertain AI outputs must remain visible and reviewable rather than being silently accepted.8. Preliminary Plan, Risks and Definition of DonePhase 1 validates the problem through short user interviews and competitor research. Phase 2 produces the SRS, personas/user stories, use cases, BPMN/activity models and NFRs. Phase 3 designs the architecture, data model, API boundaries and UI. Phase 4 implements the extraction–review–traceability workflow and one change-impact scenario. Phase 5 evaluates extraction accuracy, usability, latency/cost and the clarity of traceability.Team organization: Members will divide responsibilities across requirements analysis, system modelling, frontend/backend development, AI integration and testing. The team will follow an Agile/Scrum approach with regular progress reviews. Collaboration tools will include GitHub for version control, Figma for UI design, and team communication platforms for coordination.Key risks include AI extraction errors, false conflict alerts, privacy of meeting data, confirmation fatigue and scope creep from integrations. Responses include mandatory human confirmation, evidence-based review, data minimization and access control, prioritized/batched review, and keeping integrations optional.Definition of Done: the prototype can demonstrate one complete scenario—import a real team discussion → AI proposes candidate records → a user confirms a decision and commitment → dependencies are linked → a later change is introduced → the system identifies records or work that may require review.9. Why This Fits System Analysis and DesignDecisionTrace contains a clear business problem, multiple user roles, non-trivial domain relationships and state changes, human-in-the-loop AI interaction, security and audit concerns, and several primary business processes that can be modelled with use cases and BPMN/activity diagrams.Recommended processes for later modelling include discussion-to-confirmed-record, commitment confirmation and status update, dependency creation and blocker identification, decision revision and change-impact review, and historical decision search/onboarding.The project therefore has enough analysis and design depth while remaining feasible within a semester through a clearly bounded MVP.Professional growth: Through this project, team members will improve skills in requirements elicitation, stakeholder analysis, business process modelling, software architecture design, human-AI workflow design and Agile collaboration.10.
